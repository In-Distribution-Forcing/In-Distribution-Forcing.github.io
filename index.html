Abstract

Recent autoregressive video diffusion models are trained on their own rollouts, aligning training with inference and suppressing drifting, yet this alignment holds only within the training horizon. Test-time extrapolation has therefore drawn attention, but existing approaches remain confined to empirically motivated selection of the key-value (KV) history read during generation. We show that the first out-of-distribution operation of extrapolation is not this KV conditioning but the write that precedes it, i.e., KV caching: writing the KV entry of the last chunk within the horizon is an operation training never executed, and it occurs before any chunk beyond the horizon is generated. We term this the KV-provenance problem. A KV entry is a pair of content and provenance, the set of KV entries attended to while it is written, so the same clean chunk maps to different points of KV space under different provenances. As provenance is fixed at write time, no read-side policy can recover it. We propose In-Distribution Forcing, which keeps both levels of KV access within the training distribution: self-provenance caching renders both strictly in-distribution, and a denoising step-level conditioning schedule further suppresses residual drifting. The method requires no training, no head or layer analysis, no cache compression, and no additional memory or calculation cost.

Motivation

Beyond the training horizon, the KV cache cannot grow indefinitely, so the oldest entry is evicted whenever a new chunk is cached. This substitution is introduced only at test time: the model has never generated or cached a chunk under a truncated context, so every chunk beyond the horizon is produced from a context the training distribution does not contain. The resulting error is written into the cache and conditions every subsequent chunk, so the deviation accumulates as generation proceeds — observed as long-horizon drift.

Existing approaches respond by asking which information should be retained in the cache, treating drift as a matter of choosing a better KV conditioning policy. Such policies can slow the degradation, but they leave its cause intact: the model still operates outside the distribution it was trained on. Our position is the opposite one — rather than making out-of-distribution operation less damaging, we ask that test-time extrapolation remain an in-distribution operation in the first place.

Method

Overview of ID-Forcing. ID-Forcing controls both KV caching and KV conditioning so that every entry written and read beyond the training horizon matches the provenance the model was trained on.

The KV-Provenance Problem

A KV entry is determined not only by the chunk it encodes, but by its provenance — the set of prior entries it attended to when cached. The same clean chunk therefore maps to different points in KV space under different caching windows, and training supervises only one of them. Writing an entry under an unseen provenance yields an out-of-distribution state before any chunk beyond the horizon is sampled — and since provenance is fixed at KV caching, no downstream conditioning policy can recover from it.

The First Chunk as a Persistent Condition

The first chunk does not share the distribution of any later chunk: its opening latent frame is encoded from a single pixel frame, while every subsequent frame aggregates multiple frames. Its KV entry is therefore not substitutable by any later one, and evicting it is itself a departure from the training distribution. ID-Forcing keeps this entry in the window for the entire rollout — not as an attention sink, but because it is the only entry the model has ever been trained to read at that position.
